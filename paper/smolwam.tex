\begin{abstract}
World-action models (WAMs) predict a robot's actions together with the observations those actions will produce.
However, the strongest models rely on video diffusion transformers that are far too computationally expensive for
resource-constrained edge devices. We present \smol, a latent WAM that we derive from the 2B-parameter Cosmos Policy by
keeping its pretrained trunk and rebuilding its inference interface: single-frame visual latents, cached text features
that preserve the trunk's attention sinks, 12 of 28 transformer blocks, and 1,008 tokens from which actions and future
visual latents are read out in one rectified-flow step. Trained in 3.6 GPU-hours, \smol\ succeeds in 95.5\% of 400
LIBERO episodes at 57\,ms, 2.3\,TFLOP, and 2.4\,GiB of GPU memory per 16-action chunk, 11$\times$ faster and
3.2$\times$ leaner than its teacher, and its world head still forecasts scene motion.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

World-action models (WAMs) predict a robot's next actions together with the observations they should
produce~\citep{kim2026cosmospolicy,ye2026wam,lu2026survey}. Cosmos Policy~\citep{kim2026cosmospolicy} builds one from
the Cosmos-Predict2 video model~\citep{nvidia2025cosmos} by treating actions, future observations, and values as latent
frames of a single denoising sequence, and reaches 98.5\% average success on LIBERO~\citep{liu2023libero}. Its
inference stack, however, was built for video generation: a 4.8B-parameter T5-XXL text encoder~\citep{raffel2020t5}, a
VAE that encodes a 33-frame clip per query, and five denoising passes of a 2B-parameter transformer over 1,764 tokens,
which cost 624\,ms, 31\,TFLOP, and 7.55\,GiB per 16-action chunk on an A100 even with cached text features.

Efficient policies usually give up the world model or the video prior: compact vision-language-action (VLA) models
such as SmolVLA~\citep{shukor2025smolvla} and TurboVLA~\citep{xie2026turbovla} predict only actions, and
Fast-WAM~\citep{yuan2026fastwam} skips future prediction at test time. We ask whether a strong pretrained WAM can itself
be made small while still predicting the future.

We present \smol\ (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). Our premise is that much of the teacher's cost comes from an interface
designed for video generation, not from what control requires. \smol\ reuses the trunk's weights, token positions, and
rectified-flow readout; it encodes single frames through a small adapter, replaces T5-XXL with cached BERT
features~\citep{devlin2019bert} padded to preserve the trunk's attention sinks, keeps 12 of 28 blocks, and reads actions
from 28 state-conditioned queries. One forward pass returns a 16-step action chunk and the next visual latents of both
cameras. Most changes break the trunk zero-shot, so we distill them with a curriculum that recovers from pruning before
compaction.

We contribute (i) \smol, which retains 97\% of its teacher's success at 11$\times$ lower latency; (ii) a method, with a
diagnostic behind each step, for deriving compact WAMs from pretrained video WAMs (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}); and
(iii) findings showing that the compressed world head preserves motion forecasting, whereas adding latent-action
cascades or visual-reward fine-tuning degrades control (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}).

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Efficient robot policies.} Generalist VLAs bring pretraining to
control~\citep{brohan2023rt2,kim2024openvla,oneill2024oxe,ghosh2024octo,black2025pi0,pertsch2025fast}, often with
diffusion~\citep{chi2023diffusionpolicy} or chunked~\citep{zhao2023aloha} action heads;
OpenVLA-OFT~\citep{kim2025oft}, TinyVLA~\citep{wen2025tinyvla}, SmolVLA~\citep{shukor2025smolvla}, and
TurboVLA~\citep{xie2026turbovla} speed them up; \smol\ borrows TurboVLA's principles but retains a world model.

\paragraph{Predictive models for control.} Latent world models learn dynamics in feature
space~\citep{zhou2025dinowm,assran2025vjepa2,zheng2025flare,maes2026leworldmodel,gao2026fastlewm}, and
GR-1~\citep{wu2024gr1}, PAD~\citep{guo2024pad}, and WorldVLA~\citep{cen2025worldvla} generate future images and actions
jointly. Recent WAMs build on video models~\citep{kim2026cosmospolicy,ye2026wam} and add action
evaluation~\citep{zhou2026tau0wm}, modalities~\citep{nvidia2026cosmos3}, object state~\citep{liu2026oawam},
geometry~\citep{lopetegui2026spatially}, or latent actions~\citep{chen2026abot}; Fast-WAM~\citep{yuan2026fastwam} drops
future prediction at test time and Light-WAM~\citep{li2026lightwam} decodes actions from a smaller video backbone,
whereas \smol\ reads future latents out of the pass that produces its actions.

\section{\smol}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Teacher: Cosmos Policy}

On LIBERO, the video diffusion transformer (DiT)~\citep{peebles2023dit} of Cosmos Policy (28 blocks, 1.96B parameters)
denoises nine latent frames of $14{\times}14{=}196$ tokens (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}): a blank frame, the robot state
$s$, the wrist and third-person camera latents $o^w$ and $o^p$, a chunk $a$ of $H{=}16$ actions, and, at time $t{+}H$,
the state $s'$, the camera latents $o'^w$ and $o'^p$, and a value $V$. Images are encoded by the Wan2.1
VAE~\citep{wan2025} from 33-frame clips, and the instruction enters through cross-attention to 512 T5-XXL embeddings.
The model is a rectified flow~\citep{liu2023rectifiedflow,lipman2023flowmatching}: for
$x_\tau = (1-\tau)\,x_0 + \tau\,\epsilon$ it predicts the velocity $v_\theta(x_\tau,\tau) \approx \epsilon - x_0$,
which gives a closed-form clean estimate
\begin{equation}
  \hat{x}_0 = x_\tau - \tau\, v_\theta(x_\tau, \tau).
  \label{eq:x0}
\end{equation}
Generated frames start at $\tau_{\max} \approx 0.988$ ($\sigma_{\max}{=}80$); the released pipeline takes five steps.

\subsection{Deriving \smol\ from the teacher}

\paragraph{Single-frame latents.} \smol\ encodes each camera image once, as a single frame, with the Wan2.1 encoder
(54M parameters). Such latents raise the teacher's action error (L1) from 0.0089 to 0.038; a 0.5M-parameter adapter maps
them to clip-mode latents and restores it to 0.0092.

\paragraph{Cached text that keeps the attention sinks.} We replace T5-XXL with cached BERT-base~\citep{devlin2019bert}
features of the instruction, projected to the 1024-dimensional context. The trunk's cross-attention has bias-free key
and value projections, so T5's ${\sim}500$ zero-padded positions contribute zero logits and values and act as an
attention sink~\citep{xiao2024attentionsinks}: truncating the context to 32 or 128 positions drops the teacher to 0/5
closed-loop successes, and restoring the removed softmax mass analytically returns it to 5/5. \smol\ thus zero-pads the
BERT features to 512 positions; six zero-initialized vision-to-language layers (width 256), as in
TurboVLA~\citep{xie2026turbovla}, let the instruction attend to the images.

\paragraph{Boundary-preserving pruning.} Block influence $1 - \cos(h_{\mathrm{in}}, h_{\mathrm{out}})$~\citep{men2025shortgpt}
is highest for blocks 0, 1, and 25--27, while blocks 3--22 are individually close to the identity. Zero-shot screens
(5 episodes each) show that removals do not compose: skipping block 1, 26, or 27 fails every episode, skipping block 7,
14, or 20 alone fails none, and skipping four near-identity blocks (12--15) together fails all. \smol\ keeps blocks 0--2
and 19--27 (0.88B parameters).

\paragraph{State-conditioned action queries.} A zero-initialized MLP adds the robot state to 28 action queries that
replace the 196-token action frame at its original positions. Beyond these queries we keep only the blank and image
frames (1,008 tokens). Zero-shot, dropping the future-state and value frames barely changes the action error (0.009 to
${\le}0.019$), but dropping the blank (0.30) or state (0.058) frame or compacting the vector frames to 28 tokens (0.21)
does, so the layout is learned.

\paragraph{One-pass readout.} \smol\ evaluates its trunk once at $\tau_{\max}$ with fixed noise and reads the action
chunk and future latents from Eq.~\eqref{eq:x0}; the teacher solves all 60 screening episodes in one step
(Table~\ref{tab:main}). The future latents form \smol's world head, supervised in training and never decoded at
deployment.

\subsection{Distillation with a layout curriculum}

We train on 68,830 observations sampled every fourth step from the 1,700 successful LIBERO demonstrations released with
Cosmos Policy, using the teacher's one-step predictions of the action chunk and future latents, $a^{T}$ and $z^{T}$,
cached at fixed noise (66\,min on one A100). As in pruning and distillation of language
models~\citep{muralidharan2024minitron,hinton2015distilling}, the loss
$\|\hat{a} - a\|_1 + \|\hat{a} - a^{T}\|_1 + \|\hat{z} - z\|_1 + \|\hat{z} - z^{T}\|_1$ matches the student's
one-step estimates to both the demonstrations and the teacher, plus 0.1-weighted terms for the state and value frames
while they exist. Pruning and compaction are separate distribution shifts, so the student, initialized from the kept
teacher blocks, trains in the teacher's 1,764-token layout for the first 1,000 of 5,000 steps and in the compact layout
afterward (AdamW; learning rate $2{\times}10^{-5}$ for inherited and $10^{-4}$ for new weights; batch 32; bf16) in
3.6 hours on one A100.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We ask (Q1) how much of the teacher's success \smol\ retains, (Q2) where its savings come from, (Q3) whether it remains
a world model, and (Q4) whether richer WAM components help.

\paragraph{Setup.} LIBERO~\citep{liu2023libero} has four suites of ten tasks; we use the teacher's observation pipeline
(wrist and third-person images at 224\,px, proprioception, 16-action chunks executed open loop), and 10 trials per task
(400 episodes). A 60-episode screening set (3 tasks per suite, 5 trials each) includes the tasks an earlier pruned
student failed most, so it scores below the full protocol. Latency is the wall-clock time from observation to action
chunk on one A100-SXM4-40GB (batch 1, bf16), including preprocessing and VAE encoding, with text features cached for
every model.

\paragraph{Q1: \smol\ retains most of the teacher's success.} \smol\ succeeds in 382/400 episodes (95.5\%; 95\% Wilson
interval 93.0--97.1) against the teacher's 393/400 (98.2\%; 96.4--99.1). We detect no difference on Spatial and Object;
the deficit lies in the multi-step Goal (94 vs.\ 99) and Long (89 vs.\ 97) suites, where failed rollouts usually
complete the first subgoal and stall on the second. An earlier student that removed only 8 blocks and recovered with
LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora} distillation on 414 teacher observations reached 87.5\%; \smol\ differs in distillation
coverage, full fine-tuning, and the learned layout, effects we have not separated. Training 7,500 more steps does not
help (94.2\%), and an 8-block variant falls to 83.3\% on the screening set.

\paragraph{Q2: Where the 11$\times$ comes from.} Running the unmodified teacher for one step on single-frame inputs cuts
latency 5$\times$, to 126\,ms, with no loss on the screening set; \smol's trunk redesign adds 2.2$\times$ in latency,
3.3$\times$ in compute, and 1.9$\times$ in memory. At 57\,ms per chunk (6.8\,ms preprocessing, 12.9\,ms VAE, 36.9\,ms
trunk) it issues 17.7 chunks (282 actions) per second; because chunks execute open loop, the chunk rate bounds how often
the policy reacts to new observations. Per-channel INT8 quantization of the 172 largest linear layers shrinks the stored
network from 1.64 to 0.85\,GiB and costs one screening episode; simulated with bf16 kernels, it does not yet reduce
latency.

\paragraph{Q3: \smol\ remains a world model.} On 96 held-out contexts, \smol's forecast of the view 16 steps ahead
lowers latent L1 by 24\% relative to copying the current frame (0.101 vs.\ 0.133), and lowers the end-point error
between RAFT~\citep{teed2020raft} optical flow to the predicted and to the real future from 1.63 to 1.43 pixels. Decoded
forecasts (Fig.~\ref{fig:world}) keep the scene and place the arm near its future pose but blur its appearance, and
LPIPS~\citep{zhang2018lpips} stays at the copy-frame level (0.128 vs.\ 0.115): the world head captures motion rather
than texture.

\paragraph{Q4: Richer WAM components hurt at this scale.} We port ABot-M0.5~\citep{chen2026abot} onto \smol\ (39M more
parameters): tokens from a frozen 32-dimensional latent-action model behind an asymmetric attention mask,
mixture-of-transformer towers for the latent-action, arm, and gripper streams, four-step conditional flow matching, and
dream forcing, which trains the action head on predicted latents. It succeeds in 53.3\% of screening episodes at
239\,ms (\smol: 93.3\% at 57\,ms). Its one-step action estimate matches \smol's offline (validation L1 0.089 vs.\
0.091), but four-step sampling raises the error to 0.155 and one or two steps recover only 63.3\% and 70.0\%; its
latent-action predictions are worse than predicting the mean code (MSE 1.15, variance 0.64), and its world head is no
better (latent L1 0.102). Fine-tuning its world head with reward-weighted denoising regression under five rewards built
from optical flow, depth~\citep{yang2024depthanythingv2}, and LPIPS worsened every image metric (LPIPS from 0.130 to
0.167--0.177) and collapsed success to 5.0--16.7\% within 300 steps, likely because the objective has no action term
while the shared trunk trains.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}

\paragraph{Limitations.} We evaluate \smol\ only on LIBERO; future work should extend evaluation to additional robotics
benchmarks such as RoboCasa and, ultimately, real-robot settings. We also observe that reducing transformer depth
disproportionately affects long-horizon tasks, with the largest degradation on LIBERO-Long. Developing pruning
strategies that preserve long-horizon control performance therefore remains an important open problem.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} A pretrained video WAM tolerates large interface and depth changes when each is distilled
rather than applied zero-shot, but not auxiliary objectives that train its shared trunk without an action anchor;
\smol\ keeps its world prediction and 95.5\% LIBERO success at a fraction of the teacher's cost.
